This work presents the development of a direct digital synthesis waveform generator, implemented on a Cyclone IV E FPGA of the DE2-115 board and controlled by a computer. The core, described in VHDL, uses a 32-bit phase accumulator clocked at 10~MHz, 1024-sample 8-bit tables and a fixed-point multiplication that converts the frequency in hertz into the tuning word without a divider, resulting in a range from 0 to 262\,143~Hz with a resolution of 2.33~mHz. The generator produces sine, ramp, sinc and arbitrary waveforms, the latter stored in a dual-port memory that can be rewritten during operation. Computer control uses the Virtual JTAG feature through the board's own programming cable, with commands transferred between clock domains by a single-bit synchronizer, and a graphical interface written in Python sets the frequency, the waveform and the arbitrary table. The digital design was organized in encapsulated blocks and verified by 16 self-checking testbenches in the GHDL simulator, from the frequency conversion block to the top-level entity, all without errors; synthesis used less than 1\% of the device's logic elements, with a tenfold timing margin. A custom analog board, with the DAC0800 converter, current-to-voltage conversion, a 1~MHz Sallen-Key reconstruction filter and gain adjustment, converts the samples into voltage; in SPICE simulation, the 1~kHz sine showed a total harmonic distortion of \(-70.9\)~dBc and its largest unwanted component at \(-67\)~dBc. \pendente{Complete with the bench test results of the final design.}
